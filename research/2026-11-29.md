## Verified figures and sources
- **Tal Barahi**: on a small island in the middle of Phewa Lake, reached only by boat; two-storey pagoda; goddess Barahi,
  "the boar manifestation of Ajima" (NTB); fairs in Baisakh and Kartik draw crowds. Sources: Nepal Tourism Board
  ntb.gov.np/taal-barahi--kaski + Wikipedia "Tal Barahi Temple". "A form of Durga": Nepal Traveller (nepaltraveller.com).
  Not used: Phewa as "second-largest lake" (one source only), any build date (sources give only legends).
- **Lumbini / 249 BC**: Ashoka visited and raised the pillar around 249 BC (Wikipedia "Maya Devi Temple, Lumbini";
  Kathmandu Post, 25 May 2024, "The lesser-known Lumbini marker stone…"). Inscription: he came himself and worshipped
  "because the Buddha Shakyamuni was born here" (Wikipedia "Lumbini"). Marker stone marks the birth spot (Wikipedia Maya Devi
  Temple + Kathmandu Post). Sacred pond where Maya Devi is believed to have bathed (Wikipedia Lumbini + Maya Devi Temple).
  UNESCO site + monasteries built by Buddhist nations: content/tours.json (lumbini-pokhara). UNESCO year (1997) left off the
  card: only Wikipedia was readable (whc.unesco.org fetch failed).
